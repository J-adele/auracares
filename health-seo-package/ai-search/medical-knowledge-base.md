## ﻿# Cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and the potential to invade or spread to other parts of the body. It arises from genetic mutations that disrupt normal cellular regulation.

**Causes:**
Key risk factors include tobacco use, poor diet, excessive alcohol consumption, exposure to carcinogens, infections (e.g., HPV, Hepatitis B/C), and genetic predisposition.

**Diagnosis:**
Diagnosis typically involves imaging (CT, MRI, PET), biopsies, histopathological analysis, and molecular testing to determine cancer subtype and stage.

**References:**
- World Health Organization. "Cancer fact sheet." https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer
- National Cancer Institute. "Cancer overview." https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer

## ﻿# Diabetes

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels due to impaired insulin production, insulin resistance, or both. It includes type 1, type 2, gestational, and other specific types.

**Causes:**
Type 1 diabetes is autoimmune destruction of beta cells. Type 2 diabetes results from insulin resistance linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, genetics, and aging. Gestational diabetes occurs during pregnancy due to hormonal changes.

**Diagnosis:**
Diagnosis is based on fasting plasma glucose, oral glucose tolerance test, HbA1c levels, and sometimes random glucose measurements.

**References:**
- American Diabetes Association. "Standards of Medical Care in Diabetesâ€”2024." https://diabetes.org/diabetes/management
- World Health Organization. "Diabetes fact sheet." https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diabetes

## ﻿# Cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and the potential to invade or spread to other parts of the body. It arises from genetic mutations that disrupt normal cellular regulation.

**Causes:**
Key risk factors include tobacco use, poor diet, excessive alcohol consumption, exposure to carcinogens, infections (e.g., HPV, Hepatitis B/C), and genetic predisposition.

**Diagnosis:**
Diagnosis typically involves imaging (CT, MRI, PET), biopsies, histopathological analysis, and molecular testing to determine cancer subtype and stage.

**References:**
- World Health Organization. "Cancer fact sheet." https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer
- National Cancer Institute. "Cancer overview." https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer
